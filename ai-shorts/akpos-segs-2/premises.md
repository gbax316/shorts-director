# Episode 2: three premises

The lines below are sketches to show the shape. Orezi's own dictation replaces them, as in episode 1.

**The buyer I judged as:** a Lagos small business owner, 25 to 45. Their business works offline, they have watched a few tutorials, and they are tired of courses that "hang you". They give a short about two seconds before scrolling.

## A. The customer who walked away

**Logline.** A customer asks Segs for his page so she can order later. Segs has none, and she walks straight to the next kiosk, which has one. This time it is Segs who brings up Orezi's class.

- **Retention device:** a lost-sale counter in the corner ticks up with each customer who walks past.
- **As the buyer:** this is the loss I feel every week, shown rather than told. Strongest on pain.
- **Risk:** the customer must stay a voice and a silhouette, not a new locked character.

- **Risk:** it opens on a gag rather than a person, so the first two seconds rest on the pile reading clearly.

## C. Fifteenth of November

**Logline.** Cold open on the future. Segs's kiosk has a live website on his phone and his first online enquiry pings in. Akpos cannot believe the student passed the teacher. Then the tape rewinds to today.

| 5 | Akpos | "All this one fit be your own. Thirteenth and fourteenth." | The same kiosk, no phone, no site; Akpos gestures at the empty spot where the phone was | cut-out from the pose library |
| 6 | Segs | "Akpooos. Register me now now." | Segs grabs Akpos's phone; the link card slides up | drawn in code (the button) |

- **Retention device:** the payoff is shown at frame 0, then the rewind earns it. This borrows the explainer grammar inside a skit.
- **As the buyer:** shows what I get before asking anything of me, and the rewind makes me watch to see how. Value first, as your Build With AI pages ask.
- **Risk:** "built it myself in two sessions" must match what the class delivers. The enquiry is shown as one first enquiry, not a flood of orders.

## My pick

**C.** It is the only one that shows the outcome in the first two seconds, and the role reversal is a new joke for the series. **A** is the strongest runner-up. Its lost-sale counter could graft onto C's rewind ("today" with customers walking past). Each premise uses all three ways of making a beat, so any of them proves the hybrid.

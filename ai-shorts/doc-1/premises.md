# Mini documentary 1: three premises

The narration below sketches the shape. Your own words replace them, as with the skits.

**The buyer I judged as:** a Lagos business owner, 25 to 45, whose business works offline: a boutique, a provisions shop, a salon, a food seller. They know they "should be online" and have not done it. They give a short about two seconds.

## The figures on screen

Each one is checked against its source, and the source shows in small type under the number.

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| 109 million people in Nigeria using the internet, end of 2025 | DataReportal, Digital 2026: Nigeria |
| 165 million mobile connections, late 2025 | DataReportal, citing GSMA Intelligence |
| 47.8 million social media user identities, October 2025 | DataReportal, Digital 2026: Nigeria |

**Left out on purpose:**
- A survey saying 85% of Nigerians research products online before buying. It is quoted secondhand, with no sample size or date.
- The 2021 count of 39 million small businesses. It is older than 2023 and the report could not be opened.
- Any figure for how many businesses have a website. No source has one, so no premise claims one.

## A. Three seconds

**Logline.** Before a customer walks into a shop, they have already chosen it on their phone. The film follows one search, from a bus stop to a decision.

| # | Narration (sketch) | What we see | Made how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Before your customer walks in, they've already decided. Where?" | Frame 0: a thumb types "fabric shop Surulere" into a search bar, at a Lagos bus stop | generated still, search bar drawn in code |
| 2 | "Here. 109 million Nigerians are online." | Fast cuts: a Lekki mall, Balogun market, a Yaba cafe, a danfo, every phone lit | generated stills with a slow push, the figure drawn in code |
| 3 | "165 million phone lines. Every one is a search bar." | The figure counts up; search bars multiply across the screen | drawn in code |
| 4 | "Three shops come up. Yours isn't one of them." | Three result cards: a name, photos, a price, a WhatsApp button. A fourth slot stays empty | drawn in code |
| 5 | "They don't come looking for you. They go to the one they found." | The customer walks into the shop that came up | generated clip |
| 6 | "On the 13th and 14th of November I'll build your website with you, live. Two sessions. You leave with it working." | Your page fills the empty slot, card by card | drawn in code, then the end card |

- **Retention device:** a search bar at the top, with the results filling in as the film runs.
- **As the buyer:** fast and familiar, with a hook that works from frame 0.
- **Risk:** "three seconds" is a figure of speech with no source behind it. The title is fine, but the narration must not state it as a fact.

## B. Same street

**Logline.** Two shops side by side sell the same thing at the same price. One gets the orders. The only difference is that one of them can be found.

| # | Narration (sketch) | What we see | Made how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Same street. Same fabric. Same price." | Frame 0: a split screen of two shopfronts, mirror images | generated stills, the split drawn in code |
| 2 | "So why does one sell more?" | A counter over each shop: the left climbs, the right sits still | drawn in code |
| 3 | "109 million Nigerians are online, and that's where they shop first." | Phones in hands pass along the street; on each screen, the left shop's page | generated stills, phone screens drawn in code |
| 4 | "One shop can be found. The other has to be stumbled on." | The right owner, seen from behind, watches a customer cross over | generated clip |
| 5 | "The difference isn't the product. It's one page." | The right shop's page builds on a phone, and its counter starts | drawn in code |
| 6 | Offer line as in A | | end card |

- **Retention device:** the two counters.
- **As the buyer:** the clearest argument of the three, and I can see myself in the shop on the right.
- **Risk:** the counters make a claim about sales. They must read as an illustration, never as data. A split screen can also look like an advert.

## C. After seven

**Logline.** At seven the shutters come down across Lagos. The customers do not go to sleep. The film follows one night, closing time to the next morning, and asks who answers when they search.

| # | Narration (sketch) | What we see | Made how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Your shop closes at seven." | Frame 0: a shutter rolls down on a provisions shop, and a clock in the corner reads 7:00 PM | generated clip, clock drawn in code |
| 2 | "Your customers don't." | Shutters come down across the city: a Lekki boutique, an Ikeja salon, a Surulere food spot | generated stills, hard cuts on the beat |
| 3 | "109 million Nigerians are online, and the evening is when they look." | Night Lagos: phones lit in a danfo, on a sofa in a Gbagada flat, at a Victoria Island table | generated stills with a slow push, the figure drawn in code |
| 4 | "At nine, someone searches for exactly what you sell. Who answers?" | The clock jumps to 9:12 PM; a phone shows a search, and the results belong to other shops | drawn in code over a still |
| 5 | "A website doesn't close. Your prices, your address, a button to order on WhatsApp, open all night." | A page lit up on a phone in a dark room: products, a map pin, a WhatsApp button | drawn in code |
| 6 | "On the 13th and 14th of November I'll build yours with you, live. Two sessions. You leave with it working." | The shutter from beat 1 rolls back up in the morning: 7:00 AM, and an order is waiting | generated clip, then the end card |

- **Retention device:** the clock, from 7 PM to 7 AM. It is the only count in the film, and it is real time.
- **As the buyer:** I feel this one. I have closed up and wondered what I missed, and it shows the payoff (a shop that sells while I sleep) without me having to believe a statistic.
- **Risk:** night images can slide into gloom. The rule is warm light, flats and shops across income levels, and nothing run down (your image rule).

## My pick

**C.** It is the furthest of the three from the skit: night, photoreal, mood. Its device is a real clock, not a made-up number. And its claim needs no weak statistic, because a website really is open at night. **A** is the strongest runner-up and would cut fastest, but its title figure has no source. Any of the three uses all three ways of making a beat, so any of them proves the format.

# Mini documentary 1: three premises

The narration below sketches the shape. Your own words replace them, as with the skits. Every film closes on your line: **it starts with creating with AI.**

**The buyer I judged as:** a Nigerian aged 20 to 40 who wants to earn from AI. A student, a banker, an NYSC corps member, a designer, a small business owner who builds on the side. They have seen AI make a website in minutes and do not know how to turn that into paid work. They give a short about two seconds, and they have seen plenty of "make money with AI" hype.

## What the films can say

**From the course page, so it is true by definition:**
- Your first website in the first 30 minutes, even if you have never built one.
- Online at a real link by the end of session one, with every enquiry saved and emailed to you.
- In session two: a look of its own, then your own small app with login and test payments.
- The Qodeup Way: plan it, build it, make it beautiful, put it online, and when it breaks, fix it.
- The Project Blueprint to take home, plus how Qodeup finds clients, prices the work and delivers it.
- "AI can build almost anything. It just needs someone who knows how to direct it."
- Qodeup's own website builds start from ₦1.5m. The class is ₦49,999.

**Checked against their sources:**

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| More than 36 million developers joined GitHub in a year, more than one every second | GitHub Octoverse 2025 (28 Oct 2025) |
| Nearly 80% of new developers on GitHub use an AI coding tool within their first week | GitHub Octoverse 2025 |
| Nigeria named a standout market in Africa and the Middle East, which added 3.4 million developers in the year | GitHub Octoverse 2025 |
| "AI and big data top the list of fastest-growing skills" | WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 (Jan 2025) |
| 170 million new jobs created by 2030 (net 78 million) | WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 |

**Kept off screen:**
- Any income figure or earnings promise. The course page says plainly that nobody honest can promise income, and the films keep to that.
- Upwork's "AI work up 60%". I could only read it secondhand.
- A 2022 GitHub test that found one task done 55% faster. It is too narrow and too old to show as a general speed-up.

**People on screen:** generated, and shown as "a builder like you", never a named real person. The businesses they build for are clearly illustrative (the course's own demo, Mama Tee's Bakery in Yaba, is one). Images follow your rule: across income levels, warm light, nothing run down.

- **Retention device:** the price tag stays in the corner while the film shows who can now build behind it.
- **As the buyer:** it pulls hardest, because I see what the skill is worth.
- **Risk:** ₦1.5m next to "make money" reads as an income promise unless the narration holds it as Qodeup's price, never mine. I wrote it that way, but this is the premise most likely to tip into hype.

- **Risk:** the door device could look like a stock advert if the doors are generic. Each door has to open onto a real Lagos place.

## C. The new builders

**Logline.** Lagos, after work. In flats, co-working spaces and cafes, people who never studied code are building websites and apps by directing AI. The film asks who builds the internet now, and shows that the answer is changing.

| # | Narration (sketch) | What we see | Made how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Who builds the internet now?" | Frame 0: a woman in a Yaba co-working space, a website rising on her screen as she types | generated clip |
| 2 | "A banker in Lekki, after work. A corps member in Ikeja. A designer in Surulere." | Hard cuts on the beat, each at a laptop: a high-rise flat, a shared desk, a cafe | generated stills with a slow push |
| 3 | "More than one new developer joins GitHub every second, and Nigeria is one of the markets to watch." | The figure counts up over a night map of Lagos lighting up laptop by laptop | drawn in code over a still, sources in small type |
| 4 | "Most start with AI in their first week. AI can build almost anything. It needs someone who knows how to direct it." | Over a shoulder: a plan, then a finished site that doesn't look AI-made | generated still, the screens drawn in code |
| 5 | "A website for a bakery. An app for a salon. A product of their own. Businesses pay for this work." | The three screens side by side, each with its owner | drawn in code |
| 6 | "I've built websites since the WordPress days. On the 13th and 14th of November I'll show you how I build them now. It starts with creating with AI." | The Yaba builder hits publish; her link goes out; the end card | generated clip, then the end card |

- **Retention device:** the map of Lagos lights up as builders join through the film.
- **As the buyer:** this one says I am early and not alone, which is the advantage you asked it to show. It reads as a documentary, not a pitch, and the possibilities come in beat 5.
- **Risk:** it carries the most generated people, so likeness drift and the income-level rule need the closest checking. Three or four builders, not ten.

## My pick

**C, with B's doors folded into beat 5.** It shows all four things you named, in that order: the opportunity (businesses pay for this work), the advantage (you are early, and Nigeria is a market to watch), the possibilities (site, app, your own product), and it closes on creating with AI. It is also the most documentary of the three, so it proves the format best. **A** pulls hardest but sits closest to an income promise, which the course itself refuses to make.

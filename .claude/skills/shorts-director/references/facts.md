# The fact gate

Anything a short states as true has to survive someone checking it, because one wrong figure costs the credibility of every right one beside it. The gate runs in the writers' room, before a premise leans on a figure, never after the voice is recorded.

Work this way. Gather candidate figures (a research worker is fine for the search), then open the primary source yourself for every figure the film will actually show: the publisher's own page or report, its date, the exact wording. Write each figure down with that wording, the source and the date. Show the source in small type under the figure on screen. Compute what can be computed instead of quoting someone else's arithmetic.

Leave a figure out, and say so in the premises file, when:
- it is only quoted secondhand and the primary cannot be opened
- the survey has no sample size, method or date
- it is older than about three years for a fast-moving subject
- it is a viral figure with no traceable origin
- its scope is too narrow to stand for the claim (a 2022 test of one coding task does not show that AI makes all building faster)

When the claim the film wants has no source at all (in documentary 1, how many Nigerian businesses have a website), the film does not make it. Reframe the beat so it is true without the number.

The owner's own pages are a source for what the owner offers and promises: the course page, the company's About page, its prices. Quote them as the owner's words. They are not a source for claims about the world.

Never let a figure imply something the offer refuses to promise. If the course page says nobody honest can promise income, a price shown in the film is the company's own price for its service, never a hint at what the viewer will earn.
